## Benjamin Lauzier

**Insight:** Marketplace liquidity depends on achieving a critical mass of supply and demand at the local or category level to ensure successful matches.

**Tactical advice:**
- Focus on density by category or geography to ensure supply availability meets demand in specific contexts (e.g., plumbers per market).
- Develop a reliable growth strategy for the side of the market that acts as the primary bottleneck to liquidity.
- Shift focus from aggregate growth to the efficiency of the core match event once product-market fit is established.

*Source: [How marketplaces win: Liquidity, growth levers, quality, and more | Benjamin Lauzier (Lyft, Thumbtack, Reforge)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CYwgStMln6U) @ 00:16:34*

## Dan Hockenmaier

> "For a marketplace, you're like messing with this ecosystem that you don't actually really understand how it works. And sometimes you might do something over here which drives this long-term effect two months later, and then you're going to be pulling your hair out two months later trying to figure out what you did over here that made that thing happen."

**Insight:** Marketplace health is determined by complex ecosystem interactions where small changes can lead to unpredictable, delayed ripple effects across the entire platform.

**Tactical advice:**
- Adopt a 'gardener' mindset by using a light touch when making changes to the ecosystem.
- Monitor for downstream effects that may only appear months after a specific intervention.
- Be extremely cautious when modifying established mechanisms in a marketplace that is already working.

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "Differences in take rates are primarily driven by three factors: (1) whether you can drive new demand, (2) how much convenience you provide the seller, and (3) the level of competition in the market."

**Insight:** Your initial take rate should be calculated as a function of the demand you generate and the operational ease you provide, adjusted downward by the intensity of market competition.

**Tactical advice:**
- Start with a baseline of 20% if you drive new demand (marketplace) or 10% if you only provide tools (platform).
- Add 10-20% to your rate if your product makes running the customer's business significantly easier or possible.
- Subtract 2-10% from your starting rate based on whether the market is somewhat or very competitive.

*Source: [Choosing a take rate](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/choosing-a-take-rate)*

> "Say you’ve already set a take rate and you want to increase it. Coming back to our formula, you have three options: 1. Increase the convenience. 2. Improve the demand. 3. Reduce competition: Tough one."

**Insight:** To successfully raise your take rate post-launch, you must deliver measurable improvements in seller convenience or the quality of demand you drive to their business.

**Tactical advice:**
- Provide additional features like insurance, integrations, or team accounts to increase convenience.
- Take on more 'heavy lifting' for the seller, such as handling delivery or course creation.
- Focus on bringing in higher-quality demand or unlocking entirely new revenue streams for your users.

*Source: [Choosing a take rate](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/choosing-a-take-rate)*

> "The most dangerous strategy for any platform company is to price too high – to charge a greedy and overzealous rake that could serve to undermine the whole point of having a platform in the first place."

**Insight:** High take rates create friction and leave a margin opportunity for competitors, whereas lower rates can secure a long-term edge in winner-take-all markets.

**Tactical advice:**
- Consider shifting some of the revenue burden to a monthly subscription fee to keep transaction fees low.
- Implement volume-based discounts that automatically lower the take rate as a seller's business grows.
- Create service tiers with lower fees for users who require fewer platform features.

*Source: [Choosing a take rate](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/choosing-a-take-rate)*

> "There is a relatively clear distinction between “platform” businesses (which make it easier for you to run your business, like Patreon and Substack), and Marketplaces (which also bring you new business, like Doordash and Cameo). Platforms generally charge 5 - 15% and Marketplaces generally charge 10 - 50%."

**Insight:** Benchmarking requires first categorizing your business as either a platform that facilitates existing business or a marketplace that generates new demand.

**Tactical advice:**
- Benchmark against a 5-15% range if your value proposition is primarily focused on business management tools.
- Compare your business to the 10-50% range if you are the primary source of new customers for your sellers.
- Analyze outliers in your specific industry to understand how convenience and demand levels justify their specific rates.

*Source: [Choosing a take rate](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/choosing-a-take-rate)*

> "Companies like Upwork, Patreon, StockX, Doordash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub take advantage of The Take Rate Formula by offering different take rates for more or less Convenience and Demand."

**Insight:** Tiered pricing structures allow a platform to maximize revenue by mapping different take rates to the specific level of service or demand each seller segment consumes.

**Tactical advice:**
- Structure tiers that offer lower fees in exchange for fewer platform services or less demand generation.
- Use volume-based tiers to incentivize and reward the highest-performing sellers on the platform.
- Apply the take rate formula independently to different service levels to ensure each tier remains competitive yet profitable.

*Source: [Choosing a take rate](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/choosing-a-take-rate)*

> "We crafted a strategy that would transition the Airbnb marketplace from a “request to book” model to an “instant book” model. A world where guests could book available homes instantly. Though only 5% of bookings were “instant” that point, we set an ambitious north-star goal: 100% Instant Book."

**Insight:** Transitioning a marketplace requires identifying the core friction point for users and setting an ambitious, singular goal to drive the shift in behavior.

**Tactical advice:**
- Step back and perform a thought exercise on what the "ideal" user experience would look like if you started over.
- Define a clear vision statement (e.g., "If you see it, you can book it") to align the team around the transition.
- Address both sides of the marketplace by giving providers the tools to succeed and encouraging consumers to choose the new model.

> "The most fundamental job of a marketplace is to efficiently match supply with demand. If you can’t do this reliably (i.e. fill-rate) or quickly (i.e. time-to-match), you’ll fail."

**Insight:** Marketplaces thrive on certainty; failing to provide high fill rates or fast matching times leads to users abandoning the platform for more reliable alternatives.

**Tactical advice:**
- Focus on fill-rate and time-to-match as your core indicators of marketplace health.
- Start with a narrow vertical focus to aggregate supply and demand more effectively.
- Guarantee supply for specific categories early on to build user certainty in a vacuum of brand trust.

> "Rooted in the challenge of offering better/cheaper products to customers is both (1) making sure your supply earns enough to make it worth their while, and (2) at the same time extracting enough value to build a profitable business."

**Insight:** Marketplace profitability depends on balancing competitive pricing for demand with adequate compensation to keep the supply side engaged.

**Tactical advice:**
- Transition from flat fees to variable pricing if your service has wide variances in underlying costs.
- Avoid pricing adjustments that extract value for the business at the cost of diminishing the value proposition for customers.
- Ensure unit economics are prioritized early, as they are often more complex in marketplaces than in pure software businesses.

## Noam Lovinsky

**Insight:** Transitioning from a high-friction request model to an instant model can significantly accelerate marketplace transaction speed and user satisfaction.

**Tactical advice:**
- Identify steps in the user journey that require manual intervention or long wait times.
- Automate the matching process between supply and demand to reduce friction.
- Shift the product focus from 'requesting' a service to 'instantly' booking it.

## Ramesh Johari

> "In their example, as they evolved, they actually shifted their monetization model away from billing specifically for this friction of allowing you to pay with credit cards, instead to now billing for how you were interviewing and contacting sitters."

**Insight:** Monetization strategies should evolve from charging for basic utility to charging for discovery and matching as the marketplace matures.

**Tactical advice:**
- Start by monetizing a high-friction utility like payments to build initial revenue.
- Transition your pricing model to capture the value of matching and discovery once liquidity is established.
